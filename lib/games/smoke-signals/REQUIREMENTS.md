# Smoke Signals — Requirements & Rules

A campfire-themed reskin of **Love Letter: Premium Edition** (AEG, Seiji Kanai /
John Zinser). The mechanics, card values, and character **names are kept identical
to Love Letter** so anyone who knows the original can sit down and play
immediately — only the theming changes. Each card's art is a **campfire-themed
forest animal** (see the art table below).

> Core fantasy: instead of slipping a love letter to the Princess, players are
> trying to get their **message read across the campfire** before they're sent
> away from the circle. The card characters and rules are unchanged from Love Letter.

All rules below are taken directly from the **Love Letter Premium Edition
rulebook** (© 2016 AEG).

---

## 1. Game versions

The deck is **determined automatically by player count** (matching the official
rules — no mixing, no manual version toggle):

| Version | Deck | Characters | Player count |
|---------|------|------------|--------------|
| **Regular** | 16 cards | 8 | **2–4** |
| **Premium** | 32 cards | 17 | **5–8** |

- **2–4 players → Regular.** Premium is **not** offered at 2–4 players.
- **5–8 players → Premium.**
- The version is resolved from the lobby size when the first round starts, and is
  fixed for the life of the game.

**Mid-game joins:** the player set is locked once a game is in progress. A **5th
player cannot join an in-progress 4-player Regular game** (no mid-game upgrade to
Premium). They wait until the game ends, or the host ends and restarts the game
with the larger lobby (which then starts as Premium).

---

## 2. Tokens of affection needed to win

Round winners earn one token; first to the target wins the game.

| Players | Tokens to win |
|---------|---------------|
| 2 | 7 |
| 3 | 5 |
| 4 | 4 |
| 5–8 | 4 |

These are the official numbers (the 2–4 Regular targets match classic Love Letter;
the 5–8 Premium game is a flat 4).

Because the Premium cards (Bishop, Constable, Jester) can grant tokens **outside of
winning a round**, a player can win the game without ever winning a round, and two
players can hit the target at once — see §5.

---

## 3. Setup (per round)

**Regular (2–4):** assemble the 16-card deck (see §6). Shuffle. **Remove the top
card face-down**, out of the game for the round (unseen). **In a 2-player game only,
remove 3 more cards face-up** — visible to all, not used this round. Each player
draws 1 card (their secret hand).

**Premium (5–8):** assemble all **32 cards**. Shuffle. **Remove the top card
face-down** (unseen). **No face-up cards.** Each player draws 1 card.

First player: whoever most recently sat by a real campfire (ties → youngest). In
later rounds, the **previous round's winner** goes first.

---

## 4. Turn structure

On your turn:

1. **Draw** the top card (you now hold 2).
2. **Choose one** of the 2 cards to **discard face-up** in front of you, and **apply
   its effect** — you must apply it even if it hurts you.
3. Keep discards overlapped/ordered in front of each player (they're public memory).
4. Turn passes clockwise (to the left).

Rules that apply across turns:

- **Knocked out:** discard your hand face-up (effect **not** applied) and sit out
  the rest of the round.
- **Countess rule:** if you hold the **Countess** together with the **King** or
  **Prince**, you must discard the Countess (you needn't reveal the other card).
- **No valid target:** if a card needs you to choose a player but none can be chosen
  (all others Handmaid-protected / restricted by Sycophant), the card is discarded
  **without effect**. For Prince/Handmaid self-target cases, you must choose
  yourself if possible.
- **Naming a number (32-card game):** when an effect names a number, it applies to
  **all cards with that number**. e.g. Guard naming "4" threatens both Handmaid and
  Sycophant.

---

## 5. Round end, game end & tiebreaks

A round ends when **either**:

- **Only one player remains** → that player wins the round, **or**
- **The deck is empty at the end of a turn** → all surviving players reveal hands;
  **highest value wins**.

**Deck-out tiebreaks:**

1. Tie on hand value → highest **sum of discard-pile values** wins (this is what
   makes the **Count** matter — it adds to your *hand* value here).
2. Still tied → **all tied players win** the round (each gets a token).

**Awards:** round winner(s) gain 1 token. **Bishop / Constable / Jester** can grant
extra tokens (see §7) — including to players who didn't win the round.

**Game end:** first to the token target (§2) wins. A token gained mid-round via
Bishop/Constable that reaches the target **wins immediately**. If two+ players reach
the target simultaneously, **the tied players play another round** to decide the
winner.

After each round: reshuffle the full deck, re-run setup, previous winner goes first.

### 6.1 Art direction

The 17 character **card faces use raster portraits, not hand-coded SVG.** Hand-built
SVG (the style of [Mascots.tsx](../../../components/Mascots.tsx) /
[Campfire.tsx](../../../components/Campfire.tsx)) is great for a single flat mascot
but can't carry 17 detailed, painterly portraits.

- **Hybrid card = raster portrait + SVG/CSS chrome.** The image is *only* the animal
  illustration. The card **frame/border, the value number (corner), the character
  name banner, and a campfire accent glow** are rendered as SVG/CSS *on top*, so the
  gameplay-critical number/name stay crisp at any size and reuse the per-game
  `accent` colors.
- **Dual theme — campfire + royal.** A woodland *royal court gathered around the
  fire*: animals in regalia, warm firelight palette, drifting embers.
- **Regalia signals rank.** Dress scales with card value so "more royal = higher
  number = closer to the Princess" reads at a glance:
  - **0–2 (common):** plain/servant or clergy garb — Assassin (hooded cloak),
    Jester (motley), Guard (leather + spear), Priest (humble robes), Cardinal
    (ornate vestments + mitre).
  - **3–5 (court):** nobles' doublets/gowns, light circlets, medallions — Baron,
    Baroness, Handmaid (servant), Sycophant (gaudy climber), Prince (circlet),
    Count (courtier).
  - **6–9 (royalty/high office):** crowns and full regalia — King (crown), Constable
    (captain's armor + sash), Countess (tiara), Dowager Queen (queen's crown),
    Princess (flower-and-ember crown, the centerpiece), Bishop (grand mitre +
    crosier).
- **The hub game-card mascot stays SVG.** The hatted bear in
  [Mascots.tsx](../../../components/Mascots.tsx) is unchanged so Smoke Signals still
  matches its sibling game cards on the hub. (We can reuse the existing owl / fox /
  raccoon / fish silhouettes as *inspiration* for those card portraits, but the
  card faces themselves are new raster art.)

### 6.2 Asset pipeline

- **Location:** `public/games/smoke-signals/cards/<slug>.webp` with a `.png`
  fallback; `<slug>` = card id (`assassin`, `jester`, `guard`, `priest`, `cardinal`,
  `baron`, `baroness`, `handmaid`, `sycophant`, `prince`, `count`, `king`,
  `constable`, `countess`, `dowager-queen`, `princess`, `bishop`) — 17 files.
- **Format/size:** portrait **5:7** aspect, exported **@2x** for retina; WebP primary,
  PNG fallback; served via `next/image`; off-screen cards lazy-loaded.
- **Manifest:** `cards.ts` maps each `CardId` → `{ value, name, animal, art }` so the
  card component pulls the right portrait + chrome from one source of truth.
- **Card back:** one shared back image (woodland crest by firelight) + SVG chrome.

## 7. Special rules summary

- **Assassin (0):** anti-Guard, only triggers while held. Forces a redraw after.
- **Jester (0):** bet on the round winner; pays a bonus token if right.
- **Count (5):** discard-pile value booster for the deck-out comparison; stacks.
- **Constable (6):** rewards being knocked out while he's in your discard.
- **Sycophant (4):** forces the next targeting card to include your pick.
- **Cardinal (2):** swap two players' hands + peek; needs ≥2 valid targets.
- **Bishop (9):** correct value-guess grants a token (can win the game) without
  eliminating; value 9 everywhere **except** loses to the Princess at round end.
- **Countess (7):** forced discard if held with King/Prince.
- **Princess (8):** self-elimination on any discard; suppresses the rest of the
  discarding card's effect but not knockout-triggered effects.
- **Empty-deck draws** (Prince target, Assassin redraw): draw the face-down card
  removed during setup.

## 8. Implementation requirements

Fits the existing engine pattern (`createRoom` / `sanitize` / generic state route):

- **Files:** `lib/games/smoke-signals/{meta.ts ✅, types.ts, logic.ts, cards.ts}`.
  Register the runtime module in [lib/games/modules.ts](../modules.ts) and flip
  `meta.status` from `coming-soon` → `live`.
- **Room/game state (`game` payload):**
  - `version: 'regular' | 'premium'` (derived from player count at start),
    `tokensToWin: number`
  - `deck: CardId[]` (hidden), `removed: CardId` (hidden, drawn when deck empties),
    `faceUp: CardId[]` (Regular 2-player only)
  - per-player: `hand: CardId[]`, `discard: CardId[]`, `tokens: number`,
    `isOut: boolean`, `protectedUntil?` (Handmaid)
  - round-scoped: `jesterBets: Record<bettorId, targetId>`,
    `sycophantTarget?: playerId` (clears when the next targeting card resolves)
  - `turnOrder`, `currentTurn`, `roundNumber`, `phase`:
    `lobby → playing → round-end → game-end`
- **`sanitize` must become viewer-aware** (confirmed against the realtime stack —
  see note below). Strip `deck`, `removed`, and every *other* player's `hand`; show
  the viewer their own hand; deliver Priest/Baroness/Cardinal peeks **only to the
  acting player** as a transient field keyed to that viewer.

> **Realtime/privacy — confirmed.** Pusher here is only a *ping*: [`broadcastState`](../../pusher-server.ts)
> triggers an empty `state-update` on the public `game-{id}` channel (full state
> would blow Pusher's ~10KB payload cap), and each client refetches from
> [`GET /api/state/[id]`](../../../app/api/state/[id]/route.ts) → `sanitize(room)`.
> So privacy is a **`sanitize` concern, not a Pusher concern**. Two changes needed
> for hidden hands:
> 1. **Viewer-aware read:** extend the shared `GameModule.sanitize` to
>    `sanitize(room, viewerId)` (existing games ignore the arg) and have the state
>    endpoint pass the requester's `playerId`. Returns an asymmetric view.
> 2. **Server-trusted identity:** today `playerId` is **client-supplied** (the join
>    route trusts whatever id is sent; crew presence identity is "cosmetic"), so an
>    anonymous/spoofable id would let a player fetch an opponent's hand. Smoke
>    Signals needs a **per-player room secret** issued at join and presented on the
>    state fetch to gate the per-viewer view.
>
> Pusher *could* push secrets directly via private channels
> (`private-game-{id}-{playerId}`, authorized through the existing
> [`/api/pusher/auth`](../../../app/api/pusher/auth/route.ts)), but that's
> unnecessary and fights the payload cap — the viewer-aware fetch is the right fit.
- **Targeting/prompts:** Guard (player + number), Bishop (player + number),
  Prince/King/Baron/Dowager Queen/Priest/Sycophant (player), Cardinal (2 players),
  Baroness (1–2 players). Enforce server-side: "name a number hits all of that
  value" (32-card), "no valid target → no effect", Countess auto-discard,
  Princess self-KO, empty-deck → draw `removed`.
- **Win recording:** on game end reuse the crew leaderboard write-back
  (`crewSlug` / `winRecorded` in [types.ts](../types.ts)). Handle mid-round token
  wins (Bishop/Constable) and the simultaneous-target → replay-round tiebreak.
